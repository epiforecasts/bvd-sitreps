I. HIGHLIGHTS

3 Health Zones (ZS) out of 36 are affected, including Bunia, Rwampara and Mongbwalu.
8 confirmed cases including 4 deaths.
The second national emergency coordination meeting was held with all stakeholders around the Director General of INSP on the MVE_ITURI situation.

Context

The COUSP/RDC picked up an alert signaling clustered deaths of unknown etiology in the Health Zone of Mongbwalu (90 km from Bunia), Djugu territory in the Ituri province, which is located in the eastern part of the Democratic Republic of Congo.

A preliminary health signal dated May 5, 2026, from health personnel in the Mongbwalu health zone preceded official notification on May 8, following reports of unexplained clustered deaths (50 deaths via social media). Between April 1 and May 13, 2026, 261-280 suspected and probable cases, including 100 deaths (55 hospitalized and 45 community deaths in the Mongbwalu health zone), were reported, among whom 4 deaths were health workers. All these cases originated from the Health Zones (ZS) of Mongbwalu, Rwampara, and Bunia. The outbreak epicenter is located in the Shun Quarter (or Sun 1) in Mongbwalu (Avenues Alimasi 1 and 2).

The event appears to have occurred in a context of strong suspicion of a community epidemic, initially marked by rapid and unexplained deaths, some cases being linked to risky body handling practices and funeral rituals, and prayer groups.

Following a review of hospitalized deaths, common clinical signs such as fever, headache, vomiting, severe physical asthenia in the majority of cases, and dyspnea, hematemesis, epistaxis, and blackish vomit in other cases were noted.

Investigations conducted by the EIR as of [date not specified] led to the collection of 28 samples from suspected cases. Initially, preliminary tests carried out at INRB/Bunia were all negative for Ebola Zaire, Dengue, Rotavirus, Cholera, Malaria, Yersinia pestis, MPOX, and COVID-19, before 13 of these samples were sent to INRB/Kinshasa.

As of May 14, 2026, 8 cases tested positive for Ebola virus disease (Bundibugyo/Uganda strain) after tests conducted at INRB Kinshasa. This will be the 17th epidemic of Ebola virus disease in the DRC.

II. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL SITUATION UPDATE

From April 1 to May 13, 2026, 246 suspected cases including 80 deaths were reported, among whom 4 deaths were health workers. All these cases originated from the Health Zones (ZS) of Mongbwalu, Rwampara, and Bunia.
The main epicenter is located in Shun Quarter (or Sun 1) in Mongbwalu, particularly on Alimasi 1 and 2 avenues.

III. COORDINATION

Holding of national coordination meetings with all stakeholders and these provinces.
Preparation of the response plan MVE_17 ITURI to COUSP in response mode.
Preparation for the cascading activation of EIRs and equipment.
Preparation for the deployment of teams at all levels.

IV. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL SURVEILLANCE

Official notification of the alert on May 8, 2026, following rumors of clustered deaths.
Preliminary investigation to trace the origin of the event to Shun Quarter (Mongbwalu) on May 5.
Investigation of 3 validated and collected alerts.
Meeting with 5 Health Territories (ITs) and Environmental Health Zones (ECZ) on active search for community and hospital deaths.
Pre-listing of contacts (63 contacts) and to be completed.
Strengthening of active surveillance in 3 Health Zones (AS) (CECA 20, Mongbwalu, and Abelkozo).
Development of contact lists to initiate tracing.
Active search for suspected FHA cases at Mongbwalu Hospital.
Securing of bodies.

V. LABORATORY

Thirteen initial samples collected, 2 analyzed by rapid diagnostic test (RDT) with negative results (Dengue, MPOX, Malaria, Yesinia pestis, COVID-19, Rotavirus, and Cholera).
Biological samples from the 28 suspected cases collected and sent to INRB/Kinshasa for analysis.

VII. INFECTION PREVENTION AND CONTROL (IPC/WASH)

IPC evaluation, CS Abelkozo (score 7%), HGR Mongbwalu (score 34%).
IPC briefing: hand hygiene, waste management, and patient isolation ✓ Preparation of isolation at HGR Mongbwalu.
Implementation of Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures in local health facilities, despite implementation difficulties.

IX. RISK COMMUNICATION AND COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT

Initial sensitization of community and religious leaders to counter rumors of "mystical disease."

X. SECURITY

Daily security monitoring.

CHALLENGES AND GAPS

Lack of compliant isolation, development of the Integrated Care Plan (PAI), and designation of the Integrated Surveillance System (SGI).
Lack of IPC inputs, sample collection kits.
Need for Community Response Epidemic Cells (CREC) to manage community rumors.
Personnel not trained in IPC, sample collection, transport and conservation, surveillance, and care for epidemic-potential diseases.
Lack of triage at HGR Mongbwalu and CS.
Completion of Epidemiological Data Sheet (EDS).
Urgent need for standardized Treatment Centers for Epidemics (CTE) in the 3 affected Health Zones.

XIII. PERSPECTIVES

Organize and standardize Dignified and Secure Burials (EDS) to neutralize the major source of contamination represented by bodies.
Establish or strengthen Ebola Treatment Centers (CTE) for early patient management.
Intensify community engagement to stop mass collective prayers and risky funeral rituals.
Resolve the crisis of trust between the Health Zone and the population by providing transparent information on biological confirmations.
Trigger immediate cross-border coordination with Uganda and South Sudan to monitor population movements at the borders. Activation of the Public Health Emergency Operations Center (COUSP) through the National Institute of Public Health (INSP) in response mode, level 1 according to the operations concept.
An appeal for mobilization of technical and financial partners to demonstrate solidarity and alleviate the suffering of populations behind the government's action, as stated: A coordination team, A plan, and A budget.
Strengthening of epidemiological and biological surveillance in affected and at-risk health zones.
Holistic and free care for Ebola cases and rapid, strict isolation of suspected or confirmed cases.
Strengthening of risk communication for the disease and community engagement against this disease in a concerning security context and with a high population density and mobility.
Immediate deployment of rapid intervention teams (EIR) in a cascading manner to protect and save human lives. Furthermore, the deployment of essential medicines, safe blood, therapeutic molecules, and laboratory reagents and equipment should not be delayed.
