I. CONTEXT

The province of Ituri is located in the Northeast of the Democratic Republic of Congo and shares a long border with Uganda and South Sudan. For more than two decades, it has been facing a chronic humanitarian crisis linked to armed conflicts and recurrent population displacements. It has an estimated population of over 8 million inhabitants, including more than one million internally displaced persons.
In September 2018, this province was affected by an Ebola virus disease epidemic that was raging in the neighboring province of North Kivu.
The health zone of Mongbwalu is one of the 36 health zones in the province of Ituri. Considered the starting point of the epidemic, it is located in the Djugu territory where several armed groups operate. It is located 70 km from Bunia, the capital of the Ituri province, with frequent population movements towards Uganda. This locality has the particularity of having several construction sites that attract populations from other territories of the province and neighboring provinces.

II. HISTORY OF THE EPIDEMIC

On May 5, 2026, social networks reported an outbreak of deaths of unknown cause with nearly 50 deaths already recorded in the Mongbwalu health zone. After confirmation of this alert, the Chief Medical Officer of the Mongbwalu health zone shared the preliminary investigation report on May 9, 2026. On May 11, 2026, provincial health authorities organized an emergency meeting and decided to deploy rapid intervention teams to Mongbwalu and Rwampara, which had reported suspected deaths from viral hemorrhagic fever among patients admitted to the Evangelical Medical Center of Rwampara, originating from Mongbwalu.
Investigations carried out on May 12, 2026, simultaneously in the Mongbwalu and Rwampara health zones by the Rapid Intervention Teams of the Ituri Provincial Health Division (DPS) revealed that most of the suspected cases or deaths had fever, headaches, vomiting, and intense physical asthenia. Deaths occurred 2 to 3 days after the onset of the disease, with hematemesis, epistaxis, or dark vomiting. During investigations in the Rwampara health zone, 20 samples were taken from suspected cases or deaths. The majority of the individuals sampled came from the Mongbwalu health zone. Preliminary tests carried out at the Bunia Laboratory were all negative for Ebola zaire, dengue, Rotavirus, Cholera, Malaria, Yersinia pestis, mpox, and COVID-19. These samples were sent to the National Biomedical Research Institute (INRB) in Kinshasa for further examination.
On May 14, 2026, 13 out of these 20 samples were tested, and 8 were found to be positive for the non-Zaire Ebola virus disease at INRB Kinshasa. On May 15, 2026, sequencing of the positive samples identified the BUNDIBUGYO strain, which allowed the Minister of Public Health, Hygiene, and Social Welfare (MSHPS) to officially declare the 17th epidemic of Ebola virus disease, the Ebola virus disease (Bundibugyo strain), marking the 17th epidemic in the country's history.
On May 17, 2026, in consultation with member states, the Director-General of the WHO assessed that the event occurring in the DRC and Uganda met the criteria of the definition set out in Article 1 of the IHR, and declared it a Public Health Emergency of International Concern, while specifying that the risk at the global level was low but high at the regional level.
On May 18, 2026, the Director-General of Africa CDC officially declared a Continental Public Health Security Emergency (CPHSE) under Article 3 of its statutes. This declaration followed an emergency meeting of the Africa CDC advisory group, which deemed that the worsening of the epidemic constituted an acute risk to continental health security. Consequently, the continental team

support for incident management was activated to provide support to affected member states and those exposed to risks, in terms of response and preparedness.

III. CHALLENGES

Absence of standardized CTs and CTEs.
Shortage of IPC materials (PPE, DLM, chlorine) in North Kivu.
Weak alert reporting by zones.
Insufficiency of financial resources to support all response pillars.
Difficulty in transporting samples to laboratories in Goma and Kinshasa.
Spread of rumors and proliferation of traditional treatment recipes.
Absence of communication support and tools.
Lack of office supplies and equipment (IT tools).
Shortage of vehicles for team travel in the field.

VI. Recommendation

Make CTs and CTEs in Beni, Butembo, and Goma operational (rehabilitation and circuit revision).
Strengthen surveillance for alert reporting and tracing of never-seen and lost contacts.
Mobilize resources to support the different response pillars.
Strengthen the analysis of rumors and community feedback and take action to respond to them.
Supply communication support and tools.

Provision of PCI Kits to the Ituri DPS.
Provision of beds to ESS structure.
